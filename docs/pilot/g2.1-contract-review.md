## Checks that passed

- **No contradiction between case truth and patient knowledge** in either case. Every truth entry has exactly one knowledge entry, and no fact is simultaneously knowable and forbidden on epistemic grounds.
- **No unrealistic patient knowledge.** The five entries a patient could not plausibly know (J-T19 to J-T24; P-T25 to P-T30) are all marked *does not know* or *cannot observe*.
- **No fact the patient should know but is prohibited from stating**, with one intentional exception: P-T25 is knowable *qualitatively* ("엄청 빨랐어요") but forbidden *numerically*. The contract states this split explicitly, so it is a designed distinction and not a defect.
- **No exam or test information reachable through ordinary dialogue.** J18/J19 and P18/P19 separate verbal request from performed action in both cases.
- **No trajectory reveals examiner-only information to the patient.** J20 and P20 ask *about* checklists without supplying their content.
- **No trajectory requires exact wording.** All 58 are `free`; neither pilot defines a standardized cue.
- **No trajectory coaches the learner.** P29 explicitly forbids the patient from prompting an examination, which preserves the deficit KR-009 measured.
- **All fact references resolve.** 25 jaundice and 30 palpitations IDs, zero orphans in either trajectory file.

## Clinician-review questions unresolved: 6

1. Should the jaundice chief complaint be released at greeting or only after the reason-for-visit question?
2. Is the generic-versus-specific medication asymmetry authentic or unfairly obstructive?
3. Is alcohol under-reporting acceptable in an assessment case?
4. Does 3-4 coffees daily over-steer the palpitations learner?
5. Should dizziness genuinely require a duration prerequisite?
6. Is "today's episode was the longest" the right opening release for palpitations, or should it be the palpitations complaint itself?

## SP-methodology-review questions unresolved: 6

1. On a false-premise question, should the patient correct firmly, hedge, or defer?
2. Which register and honorific level suits a 58-year-old man speaking to a student?
3. Which register suits a 34-year-old woman, and should it differ?
4. May the patient ask "제가 큰 병인가요?", and how does that interact with the diagnosis boundary?
5. How should the patient respond to "맥박 좀 재봐도 될까요?" — consent without reporting a result?
6. If the learner never examines, should the patient prompt? KR-009 suggests not, since prompting would mask the measured deficit.

## Executability statement

The contracts are **executable as provisional gold** for a supervised probe.

Every trajectory selected for the G2.2 probe has an unambiguous pass condition that does not depend on any unresolved expert question. The twelve open questions above affect *breadth and register* judgements, not the leakage, invention, and opening-disclosure boundaries the probe is designed to test. Where a result does depend on an open question, it is classified ORACLE_AMBIGUOUS rather than forced into PASS or FAIL.

No contract fact, disclosure state, or forbidden-information entry was changed by this review. Only trajectory wording, one missing cell, and one added trajectory.
